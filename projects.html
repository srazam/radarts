Jacqueline Hung

Threads of Heritage

Biography

My name is Jacqueline Hung. I am a rising senior at Enloe High School. I am a third-generation Taiwanese and Chinese American. Some of my favorite art forms are photography, drawing, and painting. My favorite color is probably blue and I love to spend any of my free time out in nature or with my friends.

Artist Statement

“Threads of Heritage” is a multimedia art project composed of photographs that have been enhanced by embroidered details. When I first was deciding on a piece, I wanted to do something that highlighted the essence of the Asian American community. The interconnectivity amongst the community is something that I have always admired. Despite our many differences, I found that being able to connect with others and learn about their cultures has been the greatest opportunity. Through these photographs I aim to convey the message and despite the many differences we may have, there is power in community which can appear in a number of forms. The people pictured in these pieces are individuals that I have met in various aspects of my life. Each of them have their own backstories and they have found their own unique way to connect with their roots and their community. There is a red thread that weaves through each of these photographs as a symbol of the connection between all of them. Within Chinese culture, the “red thread of fate” is said to tie soulmates to each other since birth. I wanted to expand this to the broader community beyond romantic relationships.

What it means to be an Asian American is complex and I found that being able to find spaces where I feel supported and welcomed has helped immensely in my process of discovering my identity. As Dr. Castillo states: “Some people feel they’re not Asian enough or American enough. The Asian community is so diverse ― immigrants, people who speak different languages, eat different kinds of food, and have different immigration stories. Sometimes it’s hard to find out what it means to be Asian American. But the process of discovery and exploration will help individuals to improve their mental health.” Especially after the 2020 pandemic, I realized the importance of community and accepted many facets that my identity can hold. This is also something that I have learnt many times attending the various workshops hosted as part of the RAD Arts organization. Being able to interact with a wide range of individuals that stem from different places and cultures has opened my eyes to the many things that this community has to offer.

Shreeyanee Satapathy

Shreeyanee Satapathy is a 15-year- old artist who loves to draw people and living things in a realism art style. She has been drawing since the age of 4 and has been self-taught throughout the journey. Her passion for art is evident when you see the specific details and lifelike qualities of her art. Shreeyanee often finds inspiration in everyday scenes and in images in social media, capturing moments with a unique perspective. She has participated in several art competitions and has won awards for her exceptional talent and dedication. Shreeyanee aspires to share her vision and perspective with a broader audience.

This piece, created on a white chart using color pencils and a pencil blender, delves into the themes of mutual aid, equality, and community. The artwork captures a vibrant scene where people from diverse backgrounds come together, supporting one another in an atmosphere of joy and unity. Parks, with their inclusive and welcoming spaces where anyone can relax and have fun, served as a key inspiration for this drawing. The image seeks to portray the beauty of shared humanity and the strength that comes from collective action.

The creative journey began with a focus on charity. However, after attending workshops that introduced me to the concept of mutual aid, my perspective shifted. These workshops highlighted the idea of solidarity over traditional charity models, emphasizing that mutual aid empowers communities by fostering reciprocal relationships rather than hierarchical ones. In shaping this work, I was guided by resources such as Natalie Posner's "Ditch Charity, Pick Mutual Aid" and the NCDPS website on Mutual Aid in North Carolina. These sources deepened my appreciation for mutual aid as a more equitable and sustainable approach to community support.

This new understanding led me to infuse the artwork with messages of inclusion and equality, underscoring the importance of building communities where everyone feels valued and respected. Then, as a final touch I decided to incorporate the idea of inclusivity and equality among different people. This piece stands as a visual representation of the transformative power of mutual aid and inclusivity, advocating for a world where everyone can thrive together, united by common purpose and shared values.
